UK Road-Legal Checker

Is your electric dirt bike legal on UK roads? Check the licence, CBT, registration and insurance rules in seconds — updated for 2026.

Don't see your exact model? Use Check any bike.

1. Does it have working pedals, a motor of 250W or less, and does assistance stop at 15.5mph?

This is the legal definition of an EAPC (electric bicycle). Almost no dirt bikes qualify.

2. Is it type-approved — i.e. does it have a VIN and a Certificate of Conformity so DVLA can register it and issue a number plate?

Off-the-shelf Sur-Ron, Talaria, Stark and MX bikes are not type-approved.

3. What is its continuous power and top speed?

How UK law treats electric dirt bikes

In the UK there are only two outcomes for an electric two-wheeler: it's either an EAPC (treated as a bicycle) or a motor vehicle (treated like any moped or motorcycle). There is no special "electric" exemption for dirt bikes.

EAPC — bicycle rules

Pedals that drive the bike, a motor of 250W or less, and power that cuts out at 15.5mph. No licence, tax, registration or insurance. Virtually no electric dirt bike meets this — they have no pedals and far more power.

Off-road only

A powerful eMoto with no type approval — a stock Sur-Ron, Talaria, Stark Varg or MX bike — can only be ridden on private land with the landowner's permission. Roads, pavements, bridleways, byways and parks are all off-limits.

Road-legal (type-approved)

A type-approved model such as the Light Bee L1e or Zero XB can be registered, plated, taxed and insured. As a moped-class L1e machine you'll need at least a CBT (or full AM/A1 licence), insurance, a number plate and a helmet.

This checker gives general guidance based on UK rules current in 2026 and is not legal advice. Always confirm a specific bike's status with the manufacturer's Certificate of Conformity and with DVLA / GOV.UK before riding on the road.
